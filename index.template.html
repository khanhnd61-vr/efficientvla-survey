Efficient vision-language-action research

Efficient VLA Atlas

Every paper from the Awesome-Efficient-VLA survey list (arXiv 2510.17111, coverage through August 2026), extended with the benchmark, robustness, delay-aware and compact-policy papers that appeared after it. Start from the key findings and the analysis if you are deciding what to build next.

Key findings

What the lightweight-architecture line (3.1) and its neighbors have settled as of November 2026, the assumptions those papers share, and the regimes that are still empty. The findings focus on policies under 100M parameters because that is where the open questions are.

Settled findings

  • The parameter-count race on LIBERO is over. CoRP reaches 97.0% at 48.9M, MicroVLA 90.2% at 12.4M, and MINERVA 95.1% at 0.54M. Success saturates near 1M parameters. "Smallest model above X%" is no longer a contribution.
  • Language is nearly irrelevant on closed-set benchmarks. CoRP shows it matters only on LIBERO-Goal and slightly hurts on RoboTwin; LIBERO-Plus shows VLAs largely ignore instructions. MicroVLA's static text lookup is the logical endpoint.
  • Fine-grained, pretrained, task-adapted visual features carry the performance. CoRP's controlled ablations put 14 to 20 points on each property; a hard instruction-aware token budget beats a variational bottleneck.
  • Auxiliary losses buy little. MicroVLA's own 2x2 ablation shows augmentation, not the contrastive term, closes most of its gap.
  • Parameter count is decoupled from latency. The 12.4M model is not faster than the 48.9M one; once action generation is one step, the vision encoder dominates per-step cost, and MINERVA finds flow matching gives nothing over one-pass regression at tiny scale.
  • Nominal LIBERO hides what matters. LIBERO-Plus drops models from 95% to under 30% under camera or initial-state shifts; LoopQ shows quantization losses invisible on LIBERO reappear under perturbation; TBD-VLA shows a 4-step inference delay cuts success from 97.7% to 72.3%.

Assumptions the compact-policy papers share

  • Efficiency means parameters, plus a synthetic-input latency on a desktop GPU; quality means in-distribution LIBERO success.
  • The encoder must be fine-tuned per task set. CoRP: freezing DINOv2 costs 19.8 points. Patch Policy reports the opposite with dense frozen patches, but only on LIBERO-Goal. The contradiction is unresolved.
  • Pretrained initialization is decisive. CoRP: a random ViT-S gets 78%. MINERVA: a 0.54M model trained from scratch gets 95%. Also unresolved; the difference is somewhere in resolution, architecture bias, token budget or augmentation.
  • Same compute every control step, single-frame Markov policies, no temporal reuse at tiny scale.
  • Deployment is measured open-loop on a workstation. MicroVLA's "CPU" is a Threadripper; no int8 path; no manipulation policy has been run closed-loop on a microcontroller.

Open regimes and crowded ones

  • Open: microcontroller-class closed-loop control. Nothing found. TinyVLM puts an 892 KB distilled encoder on an MCU for detection only. The research question is whether the pretraining benefit CoRP calls decisive can be distilled into a sub-500K integer encoder.
  • Open: the two contradictions above (frozen vs. fine-tuned; pretrained vs. scratch), each resolvable in a few GPU-days with a controlled 2x2.
  • Crowded: delay-aware training (Action ControlNet, DEFLECT, training-time RTC, RL for real-time VLAs).
  • Crowded: action-conditioned token selection (ActPrune, ADP, Action-JND, ActiveVLA) and learned caching (LAC, gated VLA-Cache, temporal token reuse).
  • Crowded: continual learning on LIBERO (CLARE, LifelongVLA, CRL-VLA, DMPEL) and viewpoint robustness (gripper-anchored tokens, calibration-free view-robust VLA).
